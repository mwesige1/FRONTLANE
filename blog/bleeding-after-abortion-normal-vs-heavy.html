Bleeding After Abortion: What Is Normal and What Is Not

📅 Updated: 2 October 2026⏱ 3 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our medical Team

Bleeding is expected after an abortion. The question is how much, and for how long. Here is how to tell normal bleeding from bleeding that needs help.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for education and does not replace advice from a doctor. Read our medical disclaimer.

What is normal

  • Bleeding heavier than a period for several hours or days, with some clots
  • Bleeding that gradually becomes lighter
  • Light bleeding, spotting or brown discharge for up to about two weeks
  • Spotting that stops and starts

Warning signs of heavy bleeding

  • Soaking through more than two pads in an hour
  • Very large clots, or bleeding that becomes heavier after it had lightened
  • Dizziness, fainting, a racing heart or feeling very weak
  • Fever or foul-smelling discharge with the bleeding

What to do

If you have any of these signs, call +256 759 983 865 or go to the nearest hospital immediately. Do not wait to see if it settles. Note how many pads you use and when, as this helps the doctor. See emergency care.

Helpful tips

Use pads, not tampons, so you can see how much you are bleeding. Keep your follow-up visit even if you feel well.

Frequently asked questions

How long can bleeding last?

Light bleeding or spotting can continue for up to about two weeks. Bleeding that is heavy or getting heavier needs care.

Is passing clots normal?

Some clots are normal, but very large clots or heavy bleeding need urgent attention.

Can bleeding stop and start again?

Yes, spotting can come and go. Tell your doctor if it becomes heavy or smells unpleasant.
