Contraception Methods in Uganda: A Simple Guide

📅 Updated: 2 October 2026⏱ 3 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our medical Team

Contraception lets you decide if and when to have a child. There are many methods, and the best one depends on your body, your life and your preferences.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for education and does not replace advice from a doctor. Read our medical disclaimer.

Common methods

MethodHow it worksHow long
Contraceptive pillA daily tabletTaken every day
InjectionA hormone injectionAbout every three months
ImplantA small rod under the skin of the armSeveral years
IUD (coil)Copper or hormonal device in the wombMany years
CondomsA barrier worn during sex; also protect against infectionsEach time
Emergency contraceptionUsed after unprotected sexOne-time use
SterilisationA permanent procedurePermanent

How to choose

  • How long you want protection
  • Side effects and how it may change your periods
  • Your health and any medicines you take
  • Privacy and how easy it is to use
  • Whether you also need protection from infections

After an abortion or miscarriage

You can start contraception straight away, and it is wise to, as fertility returns quickly. See family planning at Frontlane and our family planning guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which method is the most effective?

Implants and IUDs are among the most effective because you do not have to remember them.

Does contraception cause infertility?

Modern methods do not cause permanent infertility. After stopping the injection, fertility can take a few months to return.

Can I use contraception while breastfeeding?

Some methods are suitable while breastfeeding. Ask your clinician which are right for you.
