How Abortion Pills Work: Mifepristone and Misoprostol Explained

📅 Updated: 2 October 2026⏱ 3 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our medical Team

A medical abortion uses two medicines that work in different ways. Here is how they work and why they should be used with a doctor's guidance.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for education and does not replace advice from a doctor. Read our medical disclaimer.

The two medicines

Mifepristone blocks progesterone, the hormone that supports a pregnancy. Misoprostol softens the cervix and makes the womb contract so it empties. Used together, they are the regimen recommended by the World Health Organization and are more effective than misoprostol alone.

What you may feel

  • Cramping, often stronger than a period
  • Bleeding heavier than a period, with clots
  • Nausea, diarrhoea, tiredness, and sometimes a brief fever or chills

Who they may not suit

Pills may be unsuitable if an ectopic pregnancy is possible, if you have an IUD in place, have certain medical conditions or are allergic to the medicines. An ultrasound and a health check show whether they are right for you. See ectopic pregnancy warning signs.

Your doctor sets the timing

Your doctor tells you exactly when to take each medicine and what to expect. Do not decide the timing yourself, and do not take pills bought without a check. A follow-up visit confirms the abortion is complete. Read about medical abortion at Frontlane.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to work?

The pregnancy usually passes within hours of the second medicine, and bleeding then settles over days. Your doctor arranges a follow-up.

Can I take the pills at home?

Often yes, after a consultation with clear instructions and a way to reach help. Your doctor decides what suits you.

What if the pills do not work?

Tell your doctor at follow-up. You may need another medicine or a short procedure to complete the abortion.
