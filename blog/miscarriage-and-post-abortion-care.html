Miscarriage and Post-Abortion Care: When to Seek Help

📅 Updated: 2 October 2026⏱ 3 min read
👨‍⚕️ Reviewed by Our medical Team

A miscarriage is a pregnancy that ends on its own, most often in the early months. It is common and it can be frightening. Many women need a check afterwards.

Medical Disclaimer: This article is for education and does not replace advice from a doctor. Read our medical disclaimer.

Signs of a miscarriage

  • Vaginal bleeding, which may be light or heavy
  • Cramping or lower back pain
  • Passing clots or tissue
  • Pregnancy symptoms that suddenly fade

Why you should be checked

A doctor can confirm what is happening, check that the womb is empty, and treat bleeding or infection. Treatment may be waiting under supervision, medicine or a short procedure, depending on your situation.

Post-abortion care

In health care, post-abortion care means treating the complications of an incomplete miscarriage or abortion, plus counselling and contraception. It is the same care whether the pregnancy ended naturally or not. See our post-abortion care service.

Emotional support

Loss can bring grief even when a pregnancy was unplanned. Talk to someone you trust or see counselling. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm miscarriage care is offered at Frontlane]

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it is a miscarriage?

You cannot be sure without an examination, so see a doctor if you have bleeding and cramps in pregnancy.

Will I need treatment?

Sometimes the womb empties completely on its own, but you should still be checked, and treatment may be needed.

When can I try for another pregnancy?

Many women can once the bleeding settles. Talk to your doctor about timing.
